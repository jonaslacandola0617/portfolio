## Purpose

Segmentation with VLANs alone doesn't stop routed traffic between segments — that's what ACLs are
for. This lab layers a standard ACL (block Guest → Management entirely) and an extended ACL
(Guest can only reach the internet edge on 80/443) on top of the VLAN lab from earlier.

## Commands Used

<CommandBlock title="standard ACL — block Guest to Mgmt" commands={["access-list 10 deny 192.168.20.0 0.0.0.255", "access-list 10 permit any", "interface gi0/0.99", "ip access-group 10 out"]} />

## Configuration

The standard ACL is applied outbound on the Management sub-interface so only traffic destined
*to* Management from Guest is blocked, without affecting Guest's other routed traffic.

## Expected Result

Guest VLAN hosts should fail to reach any Management VLAN host, while continuing to reach Staff
and the internet edge normally.

## Actual Result

Currently in progress — standard ACL is confirmed working. Extended ACL for the HTTP/HTTPS-only
rule to the internet edge is still being written and tested.

## Mistakes

Applied the standard ACL inbound on the wrong interface first, which blocked far more traffic
than intended — Staff lost connectivity too. `show access-lists` showed hit counts climbing on a
line that should have been idle, which was the tell that something was catching unintended
traffic.

## Fixes

Removed the ACL from the incorrect interface and reapplied it outbound, scoped specifically to
the Management sub-interface, then re-verified Staff connectivity was restored before retesting
the Guest-to-Management block.

## Lessons Learned so far

- Standard ACLs should sit as close to the *destination* as the topology allows, since they filter on source only
- `show access-lists` hit counters are the fastest way to confirm an ACL is actually being evaluated on the traffic you expect
